Md Tarif Raihan · Ph.D. candidate, Mechanical Engineering · University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee

Which properties of a porous metal wall set its temperature, and how much pumping power does it take to hold it there?

I study it in two places where heat loads keep climbing: turbine and aerospace parts cooled through porous walls, and liquid cold plates for data-center chips. I build the experiment, write the model, test one against the other, and only then let the model control the cooling.

How I work

Measure, explain, control, apply

Four steps run through all of my research. Each one links to the work behind it.

  1. 1 · MeasureBuild the experimentFour test rigs, four porous specimens, Reynolds numbers from 38,000 to 70,000, and infrared surface mapping.
  2. 2 · ExplainModel what the data showPhysics models checked against my own measurements. Result: solid conductivity matters most.
  3. 3 · ControlLet the model actPython control code on a LabJack drives the coolant flow; the next step is a physics-informed neural network.
  4. 4 · ApplyCool high-power electronicsThe same physics, applied to data-center liquid cooling in a $50,000 Catalyst project I wrote and lead.
